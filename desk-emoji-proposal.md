## 3. Factors for Inclusion

**A. Multiple meanings / metaphor.** DESK carries heavy figurative use beyond the
literal object, which is the strongest part of this case:
- **help desk**, **front desk**, **reception** — service and support.
- **desk job**, **desk duty**, **chained to a/my desk** — office/white-collar work.
- **news desk / sports desk / copy desk** — editorial and newsroom sections.
- **"on my desk," "clear your desk," "back to my desk"** — workload, dismissal,
  return to work.
- **study / homework / school desk** — education.
These are metaphors and institutional senses, not puns, and span work, service,
media, and education domains.

**B. Use in sequences.** Combines naturally with existing emoji to depict scenes
that currently have no anchor object: 🧑‍💻 + desk (remote-work setup), desk + 💻/🖥️,
desk + 📚/✏️ (studying), desk + ☕/🗓️ (the workday), 🪑 + desk (a workspace).

**C. Breaks new ground.** **Yes.** There is **no desk emoji and no table emoji**
in Unicode today. DESK introduces a distinct object *class* — a work/writing
surface — not a variation on an existing emoji. (🪑 is seating, 🖥️ is a computer,
🗄️ is a filing cabinet, 🛋️ is lounge seating — none denote a work surface.)

**D. Distinctiveness.** A desk has a legible, paradigmatic silhouette at small
size: a horizontal top raised on legs with a drawer. It is visually separable
from a chair (no back/seat), from a bed/couch (no cushioning mass), and from a
bare table (the drawer). It reads as the *class* "desk," not a specific model.

**E. Usage level / frequency.** ⟨SUPPLY DATA — see §5.⟩ Expected to be high and
worldwide: "desk" is an everyday word in offices, schools, and — since 2020 —
the work-from-home vocabulary ("desk setup," "at my desk"). Fill §5 with the
five required public data sources before submitting.

**F. Completeness.** DESK completes the household-furniture set, which currently
covers seating (🪑), lounging (🛋️), sleeping (🛏️), grooming (🪞), and openings
(🚪 🪟) but has **no work or writing surface** — a conspicuous gap for the single
most common piece of office/study furniture.

**G. Compatibility.** ⟨n/a unless you can show a major platform (e.g. a Snapchat/
X/QQ sticker or icon set) already ships a widely used "desk" glyph — if so, cite
it with a screenshot; otherwise mark n/a.⟩

## 4. Factors for Exclusion (addressed)

**A. Already representable — No.** No existing emoji or defined sequence denotes a
desk or a table; the nearest glyphs (🪑, 🖥️, 🗄️, 🛋️) each mean something else and
cannot compose "desk."

**B. Overly specific — No.** The proposal is for a **generic desk**, a broad
everyday object class — not a niche subtype (not a standing desk, roll-top desk,
or gaming desk).

**C. Open-ended — addressed.** DESK is not one of an interchangeable series: it is
a singular, universal work surface distinguished by exceptionally high
*metaphorical* load (help desk, desk job, news desk) that other furniture
(dresser, wardrobe, bookshelf, nightstand) lacks. The proposal requests **one**
generic desk, not a furniture range, so it does not open a floodgate.

**D. Transient — No.** The desk is a millennia-old, culturally universal object,
not a fad or trend.

**E. Faulty comparison — avoided.** This case rests on DESK's *own* frequency,
metaphorical breadth, and distinctiveness — **not** on "an emoji similar to it
already exists." (No "we have a chair, therefore we need a desk" argument is
made.)

*Not applicable (auto-decline categories): DESK is not a logo/brand, UI icon,
signage, specific person, specific building/landmark, or deity; contains no text
and no directional variation.*

## 6. Other information — design considerations

- **Silhouette:** flat rectangular top on legs, **one drawer** visible on the
  side — the drawer is the cue that separates a desk from a bare table and
  survives down to 18×18.
- **View:** side or slight three-quarter profile; keep the top surface readable.
- **Keep it generic and uncluttered:** no monitor/laptop, no papers with legible
  text (text on the glyph is an auto-decline), no logos.
- **Both weights:** deliver color and true black-and-white (not grayscale) at both
  sizes; no left/right directional variants.
- **Vendor latitude:** the proposal specifies the *concept* (a generic writing
  desk with a drawer), not an exact image, leaving vendors room to render in their
  own style — as required (requesting an exact image is an auto-decline).
